Glass theme for Settings and the keyboard

One look across the Settings app, all its sub-pages and tabs, and the on-screen keyboard, modelled on Apple Home: a soft colored backdrop, frosted grouped cards, no hard bars or dividers between the toolbar, content and keyboard. Each mockup below shows the Wi-Fi page with the keyboard open, so you can judge both together.

1280 × 800 · 213 dpi · 961 × 601 dp Android 11 (API 30) Matches RavenHome: slate + amber, IBM Plex
The plan

Two patches, one set of colors

Both changes go in as git am patches under patches/, the same way your other Settings and Camera fixes are carried, so repo sync and patches/apply.sh keep working.

Settings

patches/packages/apps/Settings/0002-Settings-RavenOS-glass-theme.patch
  1. Always-dark base. Theme.SettingsBase moves to the dark Theme.DeviceDefault parent, so Settings stays glass whatever the system dark-mode switch says. Tab and dialog themes lose their DayNight parents for the same reason.
  2. Backdrop. A layer-list of soft radial gradients becomes windowBackground for home and every sub-page. Status bar, nav bar and action bar go transparent, and the homepage's opaque main_content fill comes out, so one backdrop runs edge to edge.
  3. Grouped glass cards. A new GlassGroupDecoration on the RecyclerView in SettingsPreferenceFragment draws one rounded frosted card per preference group, with hairline separators inset past the icon. Category titles and footers sit outside the cards, as in iOS. The column is centred and capped at 760 dp so rows don't stretch across the 961 dp-wide screen.
  4. Search bar and switch bar. The homepage search card becomes a glass pill with a 1 dp stroke and no shadow. The SwitchBar ("Use Wi-Fi") becomes a rounded glass card that tints amber when on, instead of a solid full-width strip.
  5. Tabs. The Personal/Work tabs (profile_select_tablayout) and Wi-Fi calling tabs become a segmented pill control: a glass track with a lighter sliding pill.
  6. Dialogs. Opaque slate surface, 24 dp corners, amber buttons. These stay solid on purpose: without a live blur, see-through dialogs are hard to read.

Keyboard (LatinIME)

patches/packages/inputmethods/LatinIME/0001-LatinIME-Add-RavenOS-glass-keyboard-theme.patch
  1. New theme entry. Add THEME_ID_RAVEN_GLASS = 6 ("RavenGlass") to KeyboardTheme.java and make it DEFAULT_THEME_ID. Light, Dark and Auto stay selectable in the keyboard's own settings.
  2. Theme styles. A new themes-raven.xml on top of the LXX dark styles sets key text, hint letters, the suggestion strip, the key-press preview bubble and the long-press panel.
  3. Keys as glass. Shape drawables replace the 9-patch key backgrounds: rounded frosted letter keys, a slightly darker tint for shift, delete and ?123, an accent-filled enter key, a pill spacebar and a brighter pressed state.
  4. Continuous backdrop. The keyboard background uses the bottom band of the same gradient, so the keyboard reads as part of the screen behind it rather than a separate slab.
  5. Tablet spacing. Slightly wider key gaps on the 600 dp-plus layout so each key reads as its own glass tile.

Shared colors, build and check

vendor/ravenos/THEME.md · build-ravenos.sh
  1. One token list. Backdrop stops, glass fill, stroke, text levels and accent are written once in vendor/ravenos/THEME.md, and both patches use exactly those values (prefixed raven_, like RavenHome's colors.xml).
  2. Build only what changed. build-ravenos.sh Settings and build-ravenos.sh LatinIME, then push both APKs over ADB (port 5555) and restart them, with no full ROM flash needed while iterating.
  3. Check on the device. Screenshots with adb exec-out screencap of the home page, Wi-Fi with keyboard open, an app-info page, a dialog and the tabs, plus a text-contrast check on every glass surface (4.5:1 minimum).
  4. Ship. Export both commits into patches/, then a full bacon build for the flashable zip.

No live blur on Android 11

Real backdrop blur (RenderEffect, window blur-behind) arrived in Android 12. Here the frost is baked into the gradient and the cards are translucent over it. That matches the Apple Home look well because the backdrop is already soft, but content won't blur as it scrolls under the toolbar.

Settings ignores the dark-mode switch

Every variant except Frost is dark-only and Settings follows it regardless of the system setting. Variant 10 is the exception: it changes with RavenHome's night mode.

Screens that keep their own look

Setup-wizard pages, fingerprint and lock screens, and slice panels use their own themes and stay stock. They can follow later if you want them to match.

Ten directions

Pick one, or mix parts of them

All ten use the same structure from the plan. They differ in backdrop, how strong the glass is, the accent and the key style. Text on every glass surface clears 4.5:1 contrast. Reply with a number, or something like "3's backdrop with 1's amber".

The mockups use browser blur only to suggest frost. On the device the frost is baked into the backdrop, as described under "No live blur on Android 11".
